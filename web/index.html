Field notes on the carbon market

A tonne on paper is not a tonne in the air.

Since 2020, companies retired 876 million tonnes of carbon credits to call themselves greener. We followed every one of them, from the registry to the research.

Check a company

What gets counted

A carbon credit is supposed to be one tonne of CO₂ kept out of the air. When a company uses one for a climate claim, the credit is retired in a public registry, and that tonne counts as the company's.

Since 2020, 876 million tonnes were retired on Verra and Gold Standard, the two largest registries.

What research can measure

Researchers have measured how much a credit really cut for some kinds of project: cookstoves, forest protection, wind farms, industrial gases. For the rest there is no published estimate, and Netra does not invent one.

The measured kinds cover 38 in 100 of the tonnes.

What holds up

A study of 2,346 projects (Probst et al., Nature Communications, 2024) measured the real reductions behind each kind of project. Applied to the measured tonnes, about 19 in 100 cut real emissions.

The research range is wide, so every result on Netra shows the range next to the best estimate.

Has it improved?

No. If anything it slipped: from 22% of measured tonnes in 2021 to 18% every year since 2024.

Who is counting

Every retirement has a field for who the climate benefit belongs to. For 57 in 100 tonnes, it names no one you could hold to the claim: it is blank, says "Not Disclosed", or points to a reseller, a crypto wallet or a code.

The biggest buyers

Netra reviewed the largest buyers by hand and grouped them by owner. Research covers 52 of them well enough for a grade from A to E.

None reaches a B.

What they report, what others see

Netra reads each company's own Scope 1 figure from its report and puts it next to Climate TRACE's independent estimate. 14 reviewed groups have both.

For 4 of them, Shell, Chevron, E.ON and Eni, the independent estimate is 3 to 6 times higher. The two count different assets, so this is a question to ask, not a verdict.

A record no one can edit

Every published check is written to a public blockchain, with a fingerprint of the report and of the serial numbers behind it. No one, not even Netra, can change it.

It works: in a test on Sepolia, a demo claim that reused five of Shell's serial ranges was flagged about ten seconds after Netra's watcher read it.

If one serial range shows up in two claims, anyone can prove it, and the later claim is flagged for good. See the records

Why it matters now

Since 27 September 2026, EU Directive 2024/825 bans calling a product, service or event sold in the EU climate neutral because of offsets, whatever the credit quality.

Claims like that can now be checked. Netra flags them in every result and in the live registry check. Check a company
