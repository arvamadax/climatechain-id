    Hidden buyers

    Who is behind the credits? Most of the time, nobody you can name.

    A carbon credit is "retired" when someone uses it for a climate claim. The registry asks who that someone is. For more than half of all tonnes since 2020, the answer is blank, "Not Disclosed", a reseller, a crypto wallet, or a code nobody can trace. A claim without a name cannot be checked by anyone.

    On-chain proof

    Every check is on the record. Nobody can edit it later.

    Each published check is anchored in the ClaimRegistry smart contract on a public test network. Anyone can recompute the report from the open data and confirm it matches. Each claim commits to the registry serial ranges behind it; if one range shows up in two claims, anyone can prove it on-chain and the later claim is flagged for good.

    Method

    How Netra grades a buyer

    1. Retirements. Every Verra and Gold Standard retirement since 2020 from the Berkeley Voluntary Registry Offsets Database (v2026-08, CC BY 4.0), grouped by the named beneficiary.
    2. Project type. Assigned only when three independent sources agree: Berkeley's curated type, CarbonPlan OffsetsDB, and the registry methodology code. Any disagreement gets no research estimate.
    3. Research estimate. Share of credits that achieved real reductions per project type, from Probst et al. 2024 (Nature Communications, 2,346 projects): cookstoves 10.8%, REDD+ 24.7%, HFC-23 68.3%, wind in China and US improved forest management 0%. Types without a published estimate are shown as "no estimate", never filled in.
    4. Grade. Likely-real share of research-covered tonnes: A ≥ 60%, B 40–60%, C 20–40%, D 10–20%, E < 10%. No grade when research covers under a quarter of a buyer's tonnes.
    5. Integrity labels. Each methodology's status from the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles assessment (snapshot 18 Aug 2026).
    6. Emissions. The company's own figure, read from its primary report, next to Climate TRACE's independent ownership-weighted estimate. Different boundaries; never merged.

    Estimates from published research (Probst et al. 2024) applied to registry data, per project type. Not a verdict on any single project. Registry names are shown as published; a name may be a broker or a brand. Every number links to its source. Check them.
