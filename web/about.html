About

Companies say they offset. Netra checks what that is worth.

Netra is a free, open tool that takes a company's carbon credits, the research on those credits, the company's own emissions report and an independent estimate, and puts them on one page in plain words. Each published check is written to a public blockchain, where no one, not even Netra, can change it.

Companies use carbon credits to call products, flights or whole businesses "carbon neutral". Peer-reviewed research keeps finding that most of those credits did not cut what they claim. The data to check this is public, but it sits in large spreadsheets that only specialists read. Most people, journalists and even buyers never see it.

One search box, six honest answers.

1

What was retired

Every Verra and Gold Standard retirement since 2020, grouped by the company it was retired for.

2

How much is likely real

Published research per project type, applied to those tonnes, with a range and a grade from A to E.

3

What the company emits

Its own reported emissions, read from its primary report, next to Climate TRACE's independent estimate.

4

AI that reads claims

Paste a green claim: a language model pulls out who claims what, with quotes. Netra's data gives the verdict, and a machine-learning model flags unusual retirement patterns.

5

Proof no one can edit

A fingerprint of each published check and its serial numbers on a public blockchain, where no one, not even Netra, can change it. An always-on watcher reads new checks every 30 seconds and flags a serial range claimed by two of them about ten seconds after reading it (tested on Sepolia).

6

Live from the registry

Every ten minutes Netra reads the newest retirements on Verra and checks each one with the same rules, as it lands.

Who it is for: anyone who reads a "carbon neutral" label; journalists and NGOs; scientists who want open data and method; buyers and auditors doing due diligence; regulators enforcing the EU ban on offset-based product claims (Directive 2024/825).

From registry spreadsheet to plain sentence.
  1. Collect. 342,012 retirement records (876 Mt) from Berkeley's Voluntary Registry Offsets Database, project types from CarbonPlan OffsetsDB, methodology labels from ICVCM.
  2. Classify, carefully. A project type counts only when three sources agree: Berkeley's type, CarbonPlan's type and the methodology code. 234 projects disagree; they get no estimate instead of a guess.
  3. Estimate. Tonnes × the share research found real for that type (Probst et al. 2024), with the published range. Types with no research stay "no estimate".
  4. Name the buyer. 167 of the largest buyer names reviewed by hand and grouped by owner. Resellers, wallets and anonymous accounts are labelled, never guessed.
  5. Add emissions. Company figures read from 79 groups, each with its URL and page. Independent emissions from Climate TRACE's ownership data.
  6. Record. Each published check is hashed and written to the ClaimRegistry smart contract, together with a Merkle root of its serial ranges. Anyone can recompute both from the open data.

How Netra was built
  1. Aug–Oct 2026
    The hackathon. IEEE ClimateChain Global Hackathon 2026 opens, with five training sessions on AI, blockchain, ethics and good practice for climate.
  2. 20 Oct, morning
    First idea, dropped. AI forecasting of forest and peat fires in Indonesia. Research showed strong existing systems (ProbFire, Copernicus fire danger). Competing on a forecast model was the wrong fight.
  3. 20 Oct
    Second idea, parked. Fire-risk "buffer pools" for forest carbon credits. A real problem (Nature, 2026: US forest buffer pools about 6× too small), but no open map of real projects to build on in five days.
  4. 20 Oct
    The pivot to carbon credit retirement. 20 searches in Scopus returned 704 papers; 50 were kept. The strongest finding: companies keep buying low-quality credits, and research can measure how low.
  5. 20 Oct
    What the data said. In Verra's own data, no serial range was retired twice: double counting inside one registry is not the problem. The problem is what happens around it: 44.7% of retirement records name no beneficiary (57% of tonnes once resellers, wallets and unmatched codes are counted), and one credit can back several claims outside the registry.
  6. 20 Oct
    Red team. 14 weaknesses written down and fixed. Three changed the design: build on open data instead of copying it; record claims on-chain instead of minting tokens (which can itself create double counting); and target double claiming, not double issuing.
  7. 20 Oct
    The name. Working names ClaimCheck and Proofset were descriptive but forgettable. Netra was chosen (see below).
  8. 20–21 Oct
    Data and contract. Three-signal classification, research ranges from the source data, Climate TRACE ownership attribution, emissions from 79 company groups, and ClaimRegistry v2 with Merkle-root claims and on-chain challenges (8 tests passing).
  9. 21 Oct
    Brand and site. Ink, paper and one orange signal; the claimed-vs-real bar; no green, because green is the colour of greenwashing.
  10. 21 Oct
    Live. A claim checker that reads green claims with AI and lets the data decide, and a live check of every new Verra retirement as it lands.
  11. 25 Oct
    Submission to Track 1: Carbon Markets & Emissions Transparency.

Netra means "eye".

In Sanskrit, netra (नेत्र) means eye; the word also lives on in Indonesian and other languages of South and Southeast Asia. It is short, easy to say in any language, and says exactly what the tool does: look closely, and show what is there.

The mark is an eye whose iris is only half filled. The filled part is the share of a claim that holds up, the same idea as the claimed-vs-real bar on every company page.

The papers Netra stands on

Probst et al. 2024

Nature Communications · 10.1038/s41467-024-53645-z

Pooled 2,346 projects (about 1 billion tonnes). Fewer than 16% of credits were real reductions. Gives the share per type that Netra uses: cookstoves 10.8%, REDD+ 24.7%, HFC-23 68.3%, SF6 16.4%, wind in China and US forest management 0%. Netra applies the share for each project type to the mix of credits actually retired since 2020, so its overall figure (about 19%) differs from the study's average.

Trencher et al. 2024

Nature Communications · 10.1038/s41467-024-51151-w

The 20 companies that retired the most credits in 2020–2023: 87% of what they retired carried a high risk of not being real. Netra's vintage and project-type flags follow this work.

West et al. 2023

Science · 10.1126/science.ade3535

27 forest-protection (REDD+) projects in 6 countries: 68% of potential credits came from projects that did not reduce deforestation significantly.

Gill-Wiehl et al. 2024

Nature Sustainability · 10.1038/s41893-023-01259-6

Cookstove projects were over-credited about 9 times. Consistent with the 10.8% share Netra uses.

What Netra does not claim

  • Averages, not verdicts. Research shares are averages per project type. A single project can be better or worse. Netra estimates risk; it does not judge any one project or company.
  • Only researched types are scored. 62% of retired tonnes come from project types without a published estimate. They are shown as "no estimate", never filled in.
  • The registry name is not always the claimant. A beneficiary can be a reseller, a brand or a subsidiary. Netra labels these and does not guess.
  • Emissions use different boundaries. Company reports and Climate TRACE count different assets. They are shown side by side and never merged into one ratio.
  • Two registries. Netra covers Verra and Gold Standard, the two largest. Other registries (ACR, CAR, ART, national registries) are not yet included.
  • Testnet records. On-chain records live on the Ethereum Sepolia test network, which works like the real one but costs nothing.
